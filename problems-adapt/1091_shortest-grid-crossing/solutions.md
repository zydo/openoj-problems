# Solutions — Shortest Grid Crossing

## Breadth-First Search

A move costs one step regardless of whether it crosses an edge or a
corner, so the open cells form an unweighted graph and breadth-first
search settles it: cells leave the queue strictly in order of their
distance from the start, which makes the first arrival at any cell a
shortest route to it. The eight-way neighbourhood is generated by the
nested (−1, 0, 1) offset loops, with the (0, 0) non-move skipped.

Degenerate grids are handled before any searching: a blocked corner at
either end answers −1 outright, and a 1x1 grid whose lone cell is open is
a crossing of length 1 needing no moves. The search itself starts (0, 0)
at distance 1 — length counts cells, not moves — and writes distances into
a matrix that simultaneously plays the visited marker: a distance of 0
means untouched, so no cell is ever enqueued twice.

![BFS distances on the example grid; corner-cutting steps bring the far
corner within 4 cells.](figures/solution-bfs-grid.svg)

The moment an expansion would step onto the bottom-right cell, the search
stops and reports the current cell's distance plus one. A queue that drains
before that happens means the goal is sealed off, and −1 goes back. Both
the distance matrix and the queue are bounded by the cell count.

**Complexity:** `O(n²)` time, `O(n²)` space.
